THE ART-LARP PARADOX

Or: how to take something fun, put it in a gallery, and accidentally kill the fucking thing.

1. WHAT THE FUCK IS LARP?

Imagine you’re a wizard.

Not the Harry Potter kind. The kind with a six-hour costume, a foam sword, and a muddy field.

LARP = Live Action Role-Playing. People pretend to be other people, fight imaginary battles, and get far too emotionally invested in the death of a fictional king.

You’re not watching the story. You’re making the fucking story happen.

It’s collaborative, immersive and gloriously ridiculous. You’re the character, the audience, the performer and part of the world.

2. LET’S PUT IT IN A GALLERY!

Someone looked at LARP and thought: “Hang on. This is art.” Performance, storytelling, costume, emotion… sounds pretty artistic! So, white walls, concrete floors, expensive wine…

There’s a problem. The gallery has rules:

DON’T TOUCHDON’T RUNDON’T SHOUTDON’T DISTURBRESPECT THE ART

Meanwhile the wizard is overthrowing the kingdom, the elf is negotiating an alliance, and someone’s just been murdered behind the fucking gift shop.

Something isn’t working.

3. ENTER: THE PARADOX

The problem explored by Alex Brown in The Art-Larp Paradox (Knutepunkt 2025):

LARP works because people participate. But the art world often expects people to observe.

LARP isn’t an object you can put on a pedestal. It’s what happens between people: decisions, relationships, feelings, and a fictional world built together. You can’t exhibit that shit like a painting.

4. HOW TO KILL A LARP WITHOUT TRYING

The institution wants LARP to be more “accessible.” So…

1Make it shorter. People are busy.
2Make it easier to understand. Nobody wants 47 pages of backstory.
3Make it visually impressive. We need something for the photographs.
4Give it a clear beginning and end. Nobody wants a civil war until closing time.
5Make it suitable for an audience. People need to know where to stand.
LARP-ness meter:

Result: participants become performers. Visitors become spectators. The fictional world becomes a display.

Please step back from the wizard.

It looks a bit like LARP but doesn’t behave like LARP. Congratulations, you made an immersive experience into something people stand around looking at. Brilliant fucking work.

5. BUT ISN’T LARP ALREADY ART?

YES.

Obviously. LARP doesn’t need a gallery’s permission. A painting exists as an object. A sculpture exists as an object. A LARP exists through the interactions between its players.

It might be temporary. It might leave nothing behind but memories. And that’s perfectly fucking fine.

6. SO, CAN LARP LIVE IN A GALLERY?

Of course it can! Galleries aren’t evil. They just need to understand what they’re dealing with. You can’t take something whose value comes from participation, agency and immersion, turn everyone into spectators, and assume nothing important has changed.

Don’t turn the wizard into an exhibit. Let people actually be the fucking wizard.

Bring LARP into the art world without sanding off everything that makes it LARP.

THE END

So what’s the Art-LARP Paradox? In trying to make LARP respectable as art, we might accidentally remove the very things that make it interesting as art.

Put a bunch of people pretending to be elves in a white room, tell them not to touch anything, and wonder why the fucking magic disappeared.

And that’s the paradox.
